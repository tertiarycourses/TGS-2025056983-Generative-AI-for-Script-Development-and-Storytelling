# Learner Guide

## Learning outcomes

LO1: Use generative AI techniques to develop compelling script elements enriched with narrative structure and creative storytelling.

LO2: Identify effective prompt terms and narrative components to enhance AI-generated storyboards for visual storytelling.

LO3: Utilise generative AI tools to refine video scripts for clarity, tone, and narrative consistency.

LO4: Analyse generative AI outputs for ethical issues, bias, and copyright risks, applying appropriate corrective actions.

LO5: Identify copyright risks in generative AI production and apply corrective actions to avoid copyright infringement.

## Topic 1: Story architecture, world-building and character systems

### Premise contract

Input: creative brief and audience need. Control: compress protagonist, goal, obstacle, stakes and change into one causal sentence. Output: premise record. Accept when every clause must affect the central conflict. Failure: theme without dramatic action. Evidence: premise field coverage.

### Story question and controlling idea

Input: premise and intended audience effect. Control: frame the uncertain dramatic question and the value change the ending proves. Output: story spine. Accept when ending must answer the opening question. Failure: message stated before it is earned. Evidence: question-to-resolution trace.

### Three-act turning-point model

Input: story spine and target duration. Control: allocate setup, confrontation and resolution around irreversible decisions. Output: beat sheet. Accept when each act turn changes goal, cost or available options. Failure: episodic events without escalation. Evidence: causal beat ratio.

### Five-beat short-form arc

Input: single objective and platform duration. Control: map hook, trigger, escalation, crisis and payoff to timed beats. Output: short-form beat map. Accept when each beat changes audience knowledge or emotion. Failure: montage without progression. Evidence: beat function coverage.

### Character want, need and misbelief

Input: premise and character seed. Control: separate external objective, internal need and belief under pressure. Output: character arc card. Accept when climax forces a choice between want and need. Failure: traits without decisions. Evidence: decision-to-arc alignment.

### Character relationship engine

Input: two character arc cards. Control: define power, dependence, conflict, history and change across scenes. Output: relationship matrix. Accept when each relationship beat must alter trust or leverage. Failure: dialogue that repeats exposition. Evidence: relationship-state changes.

### World rule ledger

Input: genre promise and setting constraints. Control: record physical, social, economic and cultural rules with consequences. Output: world bible. Accept when a rule is usable only when violation has a cost. Failure: decorative lore. Evidence: rule-to-consequence coverage.

### Scene objective-obstacle-turn

Input: beat sheet and character state. Control: encode objective, tactic, obstacle, turn and exit value for every scene. Output: scene card. Accept when scene exits with changed value or information. Failure: talking scene with no turn. Evidence: scene-change rate.

### Scenario iteration tree

Input: approved premise and constraints. Control: vary one narrative variable per branch and compare consequences. Output: option matrix. Accept when select by audience fit, originality and feasibility. Failure: random novelty without brief fit. Evidence: controlled-variable score.

### Human creative authorship record

Input: AI suggestions and creator decisions. Control: log selection, rejection, recombination and original contribution. Output: creative decision log. Accept when human must make consequential expressive choices. Failure: unmodified model output. Evidence: documented human contribution.

## Topic 2: Prompt contracts, storyboard grammar and visual continuity

### Prompt role and boundary

Input: approved story bible and task. Control: separate durable constraints, task input, references and output schema. Output: prompt contract. Accept when untrusted text cannot override creative or safety constraints. Failure: prompt injection from references. Evidence: role-boundary violations.

### Narrative constraint stack

Input: character, world, tone and audience rules. Control: rank hard continuity constraints above optional style preferences. Output: constraint ledger. Accept when reject output that violates a hard rule. Failure: contradictory character or setting. Evidence: hard-rule pass rate.

### Few-shot scene imitation control

Input: original example scenes and target schema. Control: demonstrate format and pacing without copying protected expression. Output: example pack. Accept when examples teach structure, not distinctive wording. Failure: near-copy reproduction. Evidence: phrase-overlap review.

### Storyboard shot grammar

Input: scene card and emotional intention. Control: map shot size, angle, movement, duration and transition to story function. Output: shot list. Accept when camera choice must reveal information or emotion. Failure: random cinematic adjectives. Evidence: motivated-shot share.

### Continuity state ledger

Input: approved scenes and character bible. Control: track wardrobe, props, geography, time, eyeline and emotional state. Output: continuity sheet. Accept when next shot inherits all unchanged states. Failure: visual discontinuity. Evidence: state mismatch count.

### Image prompt anatomy

Input: shot list and continuity state. Control: specify subject, action, setting, composition, lens, light, palette and exclusions. Output: visual prompt record. Accept when prompt must encode story function and continuity anchors. Failure: beautiful but unusable image. Evidence: prompt field completeness.

### Reference image control

Input: owned or licensed references. Control: label style, subject, composition and edit-target roles separately. Output: reference manifest. Accept when do not imply permission from mere access. Failure: unlicensed or ambiguous reference. Evidence: rights-labelled reference rate.

### Iterative variation protocol

Input: baseline prompt and generated frames. Control: change one parameter, preserve invariants and score against rubric. Output: variation log. Accept when retain a frame only when it improves named criteria. Failure: untraceable prompt drift. Evidence: single-variable iteration rate.

### Storyboard evaluation rubric

Input: panels, shot list and story beat. Control: score clarity, continuity, composition, emotion, feasibility and rights. Output: storyboard QA sheet. Accept when block any critical continuity or rights failure. Failure: subjective approval only. Evidence: rubric agreement.

### Animatic timing model

Input: approved storyboard and dialogue timing. Control: combine shot durations, pauses, transitions and audio cues. Output: timed animatic plan. Accept when total duration must fit delivery constraint. Failure: pacing collapse in edit. Evidence: duration variance.

## Topic 3: Script analysis, revision and multimodal production

### Script document schema

Input: draft script and format target. Control: parse slugline, action, character, dialogue, parenthetical and transition roles. Output: structured script. Accept when every line has one production function. Failure: mixed prose and screenplay syntax. Evidence: schema-valid line rate.

### Dialogue subtext test

Input: character objectives and scene conflict. Control: compare spoken line with hidden tactic, status and emotional pressure. Output: subtext annotation. Accept when retain line only if it advances tactic or reveals conflict. Failure: on-the-nose explanation. Evidence: subtext-bearing line rate.

### Readability and rhythm profile

Input: script text and audience level. Control: measure sentence length, lexical difficulty, repetition and pause pattern. Output: language report. Accept when revise outliers without flattening voice. Failure: mechanical simplification. Evidence: target-band compliance.

### Tone vector control

Input: brand or genre samples and draft. Control: score warmth, formality, urgency, humour and intensity with cited examples. Output: tone matrix. Accept when revision must move failed dimensions while preserving plot facts. Failure: tone drift. Evidence: dimension delta.

### Narrative consistency graph

Input: character, prop, location and event claims. Control: link entities and temporal dependencies across scenes. Output: continuity graph. Accept when flag impossible order or unsupported state change. Failure: contradiction hidden in long context. Evidence: unresolved contradiction count.

### Pacing by information load

Input: scene durations and new information units. Control: estimate reveal density, emotional beat spacing and recovery time. Output: pacing map. Accept when slow or split overloaded sequences. Failure: constant tempo. Evidence: information units per minute.

### Revision diff and rationale

Input: baseline draft and review rubric. Control: change only failed criteria and explain each consequential edit. Output: revision log. Accept when every edit maps to evidence or creative intent. Failure: endless cosmetic rewrite. Evidence: defect closure rate.

### Voiceover and dialogue split

Input: visual action and information needs. Control: assign only non-visible information to narration and preserve scene sound. Output: audio script. Accept when remove narration that merely describes the picture. Failure: redundant voiceover. Evidence: audio-visual complementarity.

### Multimodal asset map

Input: script, storyboard, audio and captions. Control: bind asset IDs, scene IDs, timing, owner and version. Output: production manifest. Accept when no orphan asset enters the edit. Failure: wrong-version media. Evidence: manifest completeness.

### Rough-cut acceptance gate

Input: edit timeline and delivery brief. Control: review narrative clarity, sync, pacing, captions, mix and export settings. Output: rough-cut report. Accept when release only when critical defects are zero. Failure: technically exported but narratively unclear. Evidence: critical defect count.

## Topic 4: Bias, authenticity and responsible creative control

### Bias surface map

Input: story bible, audience and generated draft. Control: inspect representation, agency, descriptors, roles, harm and omission. Output: bias review matrix. Accept when review impact, not only model intent. Failure: stereotype reproduced as realism. Evidence: flagged pattern count.

### Counterfactual character test

Input: character and scene records. Control: change one protected or social attribute and compare role, agency and outcome. Output: counterfactual report. Accept when investigate unexplained quality or outcome shifts. Failure: token diversity without agency. Evidence: outcome asymmetry.

### Cultural specificity check

Input: setting claims and cultural references. Control: separate researched detail, lived contribution and generic inference. Output: sensitivity notes. Accept when escalate high-impact claims to an informed reviewer. Failure: flattened cultural shorthand. Evidence: verified-detail share.

### Factual claim ledger

Input: script claims and sources. Control: classify supported, disputed, uncertain, fictional or opinion. Output: claim register. Accept when block unsupported real-world claims. Failure: hallucinated authority. Evidence: supported-claim rate.

### Authenticity boundary

Input: human experience and AI-assisted language. Control: record whose experience, voice and consent supports the narrative. Output: authenticity statement. Accept when do not fabricate testimony or lived experience. Failure: synthetic personal story presented as real. Evidence: consent coverage.

### Synthetic media disclosure

Input: generated image, audio or video and context. Control: assess audience expectation, materiality and deceptive potential. Output: disclosure decision. Accept when disclose when generation could mislead a reasonable viewer. Failure: undisclosed realistic fabrication. Evidence: decision trace completeness.

### Privacy-minimised prompting

Input: source material and tool policy. Control: remove identifiers, secrets and unnecessary personal data before upload. Output: safe prompt pack. Accept when use only data necessary for the creative task. Failure: personal data copied into public tool. Evidence: PII leakage count.

### Safety and harm review

Input: story content and audience context. Control: screen self-harm, violence, sexual content, defamation and vulnerable groups. Output: harm review. Accept when escalate high-severity or uncertain cases. Failure: creative framing masks harm. Evidence: severity-weighted findings.

### Human red-team session

Input: near-final assets and failure hypotheses. Control: assign reviewers to challenge evidence, bias, continuity and deception. Output: red-team log. Accept when every high-risk issue has owner and disposition. Failure: group confirmation bias. Evidence: closed finding rate.

### Responsible release decision

Input: QA packet and named approver. Control: record approve, revise or block with conditions and exceptions. Output: release authorization. Accept when AI cannot self-approve external publication. Failure: automatic release after generation. Evidence: approval audit completeness.

## Topic 5: Copyright, provenance and release governance

### Copyright subject-matter screen

Input: script, image, audio and source manifest. Control: identify protected expression, public-domain material, facts and ideas. Output: rights classification. Accept when rights attach to expression, not a vague topic. Failure: assuming online means free. Evidence: classified asset rate.

### Human authorship evidence

Input: creative decision log and final work. Control: separate machine proposals from human selection, arrangement and revision. Output: authorship dossier. Accept when claim only demonstrable human contribution. Failure: copyright claim over raw AI output. Evidence: decision evidence density.

### Licence compatibility matrix

Input: asset licences and intended channels. Control: compare permitted use, modification, attribution, territory and duration. Output: licence matrix. Accept when block use outside licence scope. Failure: incompatible or expired licence. Evidence: compatible asset share.

### Similarity and plagiarism review

Input: draft and reference corpus. Control: inspect distinctive phrases, plot sequences, characters and visual composition. Output: similarity report. Accept when rewrite or replace material with substantial expressive overlap. Failure: surface paraphrase of source. Evidence: resolved match count.

### Style imitation risk

Input: creator request and living-artist references. Control: translate named style into generic visual attributes and original references. Output: style-safe brief. Accept when avoid prompts seeking close imitation of a living creator. Failure: signature-style mimicry. Evidence: named-creator prompt count.

### Music and voice rights

Input: audio assets, performers and generation settings. Control: record composition, recording, voice consent and synthetic-use permissions. Output: audio rights ledger. Accept when each audio layer needs a valid rights basis. Failure: voice clone without consent. Evidence: cleared audio share.

### Provenance manifest

Input: all final media assets and generation records. Control: store source, tool, prompt hash, editor, version, licence and disclosure status. Output: provenance package. Accept when no asset without owner and origin enters export. Failure: lost source trail. Evidence: manifest completeness.

### Correction and takedown route

Input: published asset and rights contacts. Control: define complaint intake, evidence hold, decision owner and replacement path. Output: remediation plan. Accept when pause distribution on credible high-risk complaint. Failure: no accountable response path. Evidence: response time.

### Platform and client terms check

Input: current tool terms and production brief. Control: compare commercial use, retention, training and indemnity terms. Output: terms review. Accept when use only tools approved for the content class. Failure: rights assumption based on marketing claim. Evidence: terms attestation.

### Final release checklist
